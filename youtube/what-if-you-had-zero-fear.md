## Description

A spider walks across your hand. A monster leaps out in a haunted house. Your roller coaster tips over the first drop. Everyone screams. You yawn. So what if you had zero fear?

Sam's fear switch is off. Speeches, exams and pythons are easy. But his fear is gone and his danger is not. When a stunt channel offers him money to walk a steel beam forty meters up, and his best friend Leo follows him up the ladder and freezes, Sam has to choose: the views, or Leo.

The real science in this story:
- Fear starts in the amygdala, two almond-shaped clusters deep in your brain. They fire before you even know what you saw, and your heart races, adrenaline floods your blood, and your muscles get ready to run.
- Scientists studied a real woman known only as S.M. A rare disease damaged both of her amygdalae. She handled snakes, and in a haunted house she poked one of the monsters.
- The amygdala also helps you recognize fear on other people's faces.
- People with no fear tend to stand much closer to strangers than most of us do.
- A broken bone needs around six weeks to knit back together, fearless or not.
- On a ladder, keep three points of contact: two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand.

And remember: no video is worth a hospital bed. If someone asks you to drop your safety gear for views, they're not the one who'd fall.

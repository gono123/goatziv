## Description

You can read the name on a plane's tail. You can see craters on the Moon with no telescope. You can read a number plate from two kilometers away. So what if your eyesight was a hundred times sharper?

Theo can see everything. But his eyes got a hundred times sharper and his legs did not. He can spot trouble two kilometers away, and he still reaches it at human speed. When a stranger offers him money to read people's bank cards, and a boy is pulled out to sea by a rip current on the same afternoon, Theo has to choose: the money, or the boy.

The real science in this story:
- Normal eyes pick out details about one sixtieth of a degree wide.
- Your sharpest vision comes from one tiny spot, the fovea, about the size of your thumbnail held at arm's length. Your eyes jump three or four times a second to build the rest.
- Macular degeneration damages central vision first, while side vision stays.
- Some rip currents move faster than an Olympic swimmer. If one pulls you out, don't fight it. Swim parallel to the shore, then come back in.
- Could eyes ever be 100x sharper? Light spreads out as it passes through your pupil, and a normal pupil is already near that limit. You'd need pupils about thirty centimeters wide.

And cover your PIN at the cash machine. You never know who's watching.
